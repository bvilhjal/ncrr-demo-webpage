Who we are

NCRR opened on 1 September 2000, financed by a five-year grant from the Danish National Research Foundation, and has since grown into a world-leading centre in psychiatric epidemiology under founding director Professor Preben Bo Mortensen.

Our research centres on epidemiological studies of schizophrenia, mania, depression and suicide, drawing on the Danish registers and — increasingly — genetic and other biological data, with gene–environment interactions as a main research topic. Today the centre counts around 60 employees with expertise spanning medicine, statistics, pharmacology, sociology and psychology.

Lancet Regional Health – Europe 2026

How common is acquired brain injury — and who needs rehabilitation?

In one of the first studies of its kind, NCRR researchers led by Carsten Bøcker Pedersen together with colleagues from the Danish regional rehabilitation units used nationwide register data to estimate the absolute risk of acquired brain injury in Denmark over 2014–2023 and to quantify how many patients live with ongoing rehabilitation needs. The study shows the power of the Danish registers to answer planning questions that patient samples alone cannot — and extends the centre's register-based epidemiology from psychiatric disorders into neurorehabilitation.

Odgaard L, Fabricius J, Kjeldsen SS, Pommerich UM, Evald L, Nielsen JF, Pedersen CB (2026). Absolute risk of acquired brain injury and rehabilitation needs in Denmark, 2014–2023: a population-based prospective cohort study. The Lancet Regional Health – Europe 66, 101693. doi:10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101693

BMC Medicine 2026

ADHD and getting hypertension treatment to stick

As people with ADHD age, treating physical illness becomes an increasing part of their care. In this multinational cohort study with NCRR co-authors (Brikell, Wimberley, Astrup, Dalsgaard, Semark and Chang), adults with ADHD were followed across countries to see how well they adhere to antihypertensive medication. Poorer adherence among people with ADHD would mean a double burden — higher cardiovascular risk and less effective treatment of it — so the findings point directly to where clinicians and health systems should pay extra attention.

Yao H, Zhou Y, Li L, et al. (2026). ADHD and adherence to antihypertensive medication treatment: a multinational cohort study. BMC Medicine 24(1), 122. doi:10.1186/s12916-026-04714-1
